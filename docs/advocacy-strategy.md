# CPD Helicopter Advocacy Strategy

## Objective

Build a data-driven public case that Cleveland Police Department helicopter operations are a community nuisance: disruptive to sleep and quality of life, costly to taxpayers, ineffective at reducing crime, and disproportionately impactful on vulnerable communities. The primary goal is to pressure city leadership to curtail or eliminate routine helicopter patrols.

## Data Summary (Aug 2025 - Mar 2026)

- **296 flights** over 219 days (~7 months of tracking)
- **374 total flight hours** (15.6 full days of continuous noise)
- Helicopters fly **56% of all days** — more often than not
- **82 separate evenings** with helicopter noise past 9 PM
- **29% of flights** still airborne after 10 PM; landings past midnight regularly
- Peak hours: **7-10 PM ET** — dinner, family time, bedtimes
- Busiest single day: **8.9 hours** of operations (Dec 16, 2025)
- Christmas Eve 2025: circling until 11:52 PM
- Weekend flights are longer than weekday (82 min avg vs 73 min)
- Nighttime flight share is **increasing** over time
- Longest consecutive-day streak: **11 days** (Oct 8-18, 2025)

## Three Messaging Pillars

### Pillar 1: "Every Other Night" — Community Disruption

82 evenings with helicopter noise past 9 PM in 7 months. Peak flying hours coincide with dinner, homework, and bedtime. Flights routinely past midnight. Even Christmas Eve was not spared.

**Research backing:**
- WHO Environmental Noise Guidelines (2018): recommend aircraft noise below 45 dB daytime, 40 dB nighttime — https://www.who.int/europe/publications/i/item/9789289053563
- Munzel et al. (European Heart Journal, 2013): nighttime aircraft noise impairs cardiovascular function; bodies get *more* sensitized, not less — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23821397/
- Saucy et al. (European Heart Journal, 2021): ~3% of nighttime cardiovascular deaths attributable to noise — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33245107/ (full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7898943/)
- APHA 2021 policy statement: "Noise as a Public Health Hazard" — https://www.apha.org/policy-and-advocacy/public-health-policy-briefs/policy-database/2022/01/07/noise-as-a-public-health-hazard
- Bozigar et al. / Nurses' Health Study (EHP, 2023): aircraft noise associated with sleeping fewer than 7 hours — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37058435/

### Pillar 2: "Your Money, Their Noise" — Fiscal Waste

$3.5M helicopter refurbishment + $100K+/year operating costs for helicopters that News 5 found "rarely fly to fight crime." No empirical evidence helicopter patrols reduce crime.

**Research backing:**
- LAPD Helicopter Audit (LA City Controller, 2023): "No persuasive empirical evidence shows a clear link between helicopter patrols and crime reduction" — https://controller.lacity.gov/landings/lapd-helicopters
- London Police study (Whitehead, 2000): helicopter patrols "did not reduce crime rates" — https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/eye-sky-evaluation-police-helicopter-patrols-london-police-service
- Swedish police (2019): "no significant effect in deterring crime"
- News 5 Cleveland investigation (Oct 2023): only one crime-related detail found in months of records; mostly special events — https://www.news5cleveland.com/news/local-news/we-follow-through/city-to-spend-3-5-million-to-refurbish-police-choppers-news-5-finds-they-rarely-fly-to-fight-crime
- Council Safety Chair Mike Polensek: "that's not crime prevention"

**Cost references:**
- LAPD: $2,916/hour, $46.6M/year — https://controller.lacity.gov/landings/lapd-helicopters
- Columbus OH: $2.1M/year operating
- Hamilton County OH: $3M/year from sheriff's budget — https://dronedj.com/2022/10/10/drone-helicopter-sheriff/
- Cleveland $3.5M refurbishment — https://www.wkyc.com/article/news/local/cleveland/cleveland-city-council-approves-police-helicopters/95-142a2d05-6393-4019-973d-8d3c6d462226
- Cleveland conservative estimate: $125K-$250K/year operating costs (at $200-400/hour x annualized flight hours)

### Pillar 3: "Who Bears the Burden?" — Environmental Justice

Flight path data shows which neighborhoods get circled. Research shows noise exposure and police helicopter surveillance disproportionately affect Black and low-income communities.

**Research backing:**
- Casey et al. (EHP, 2017): Black residents exposed to significantly higher noise levels — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28749369/ (full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5744659/)
- UCLA Carceral Ecologies Lab (RWJF-funded, $581K): LAPD helicopter surveillance shows "statistically significant increases in patrol activity and reduced altitudes in Black and Latine neighborhoods" — https://evidenceforaction.org/grant/pathways-reducing-sleep-fragmentation-due-law-enforcement-helicopter-surveillance
- RANCH Study (Lancet, 2005): landmark study of 2,844 children — aircraft noise directly impairs reading comprehension. 5 dB increase = 2-month reading delay — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15936421/
- Clark et al. meta-analysis (2021): each 1 dB increase = 4% higher odds of below-average reading — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272494421000992
- Collins, Grineski, and Nadybal (2019): schools most exposed to aviation noise disproportionately serve minority and low-income students — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31146097/
- Children need 5-7 dB higher signal-to-noise ratios than adults; second-graders' performance drops 39% in noisy environments vs 11% for adults

## Playbook (Modeled on Stop the Chop NY/NJ)

Stop the Chop is the most successful helicopter advocacy org in the US. They got tourist flights cut 50%, a heliport removed from Hudson River Park, and a 2025 NYC law banning noisy helicopters from city heliports. Their model:

1. **Data transparency** — Build the public dashboard. Make the problem undeniable and visceral.
2. **Complaint volume** — Organize residents to report helicopter noise through whatever Cleveland's complaint mechanism is. NYC 311 complaints went from 3,500 to 59,000 in 5 years. Volume gets attention.
3. **Coalition building** — Neighborhood associations, parent groups, environmental justice orgs, sleep health advocates. Stop the Chop has 50+ member organizations.
4. **Media** — The News 5 story is already sympathetic. Give journalists the dashboard and the data. Make it easy to write the story.
5. **Legislative targets** — City council members, specifically the safety committee. Polensek is already skeptical. Give him ammunition.
6. **Concrete alternative** — TBD. Need to think carefully about what the "ask" is. (Note: drone replacement is one option per Hamilton County model, but raises its own surveillance concerns. Revisit this.)

## Technical Deliverables

### 1. Public Dashboard Website
The central deliverable. A single URL that tells the story.

**Key visualizations:**
- Hero stat bar: "374 hours of helicopter noise" / "82 nights past 9 PM" / cost estimate
- Calendar heatmap: every day colored by hours of flight activity
- Time-of-day chart: hourly distribution with sleep-hours zone overlay
- Flight path heat map: telemetry GPS points over Cleveland map showing which neighborhoods get circled
- "Was that a helicopter?" lookup: date/time search for residents to confirm what they heard
- Cost ticker: running estimate of taxpayer money spent
- Research/citations page

**Data source:** Parquet files published to GitHub repo daily by automated pipeline. Frontend reads directly from these — no database access needed.

**Framework TBD** — could be Observable Framework, Next.js, Astro, or static HTML + Plotly/deck.gl. Needs designer input on look and feel.

### 2. Shareable Social Assets
Auto-generated or templated cards for social media: "Last night: CPD helicopter circled for 2 hours, landing at 11:47 PM. Cost to you: ~$800."

### 3. Cost Model
Transparent calculator on the site with stated assumptions. Let people see and challenge the math.

### 4. Research/Advocacy Page
Curated citations organized by pillar with plain-language summaries. Not an academic paper — a persuasion tool.
